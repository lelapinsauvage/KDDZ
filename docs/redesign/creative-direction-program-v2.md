# Kiddz Online Creative Direction Program V2

**Status:** Active decision program
**Started:** 2026-07-13
**Decision owner:** Product owner
**Production redesign:** Paused until a direction is explicitly locked
**Working surface:** `/design-lab/brand-directions`
**Finalist identity proof:** `/design-lab/brand-directions/identity-worlds`

## Why The Direction Gate Was Reset

The first territory round proved that the same operational truth could survive three visual treatments, but it did not create enough real divergence. Daylight, Signal, and Carebook changed temperature, type, and density while retaining almost the same white SaaS shell. The provisional Daylight recommendation was rejected by the product owner. It is historical evidence, not an active recommendation.

The new gate begins one level earlier. It compares six complete brand systems before choosing card styling or redesigning production pages. Each system must make a different strategic bet and express it consistently through identity, typography, color, image, layout, voice, motion, and product behavior.

## The Decision We Are Making

We are not choosing a dashboard skin. We are choosing the system that should make Kiddz Online recognizable when the logo is absent and credible when the interface is dense.

The selected direction must answer:

1. What should a nursery manager believe about Kiddz after 30 seconds?
2. Which category convention are we rejecting, and what do we own instead?
3. Which two or three assets can become unmistakably Kiddz over time?
4. How does the brand behave in a calm moment, an urgent moment, and a completed moment?
5. Can the system survive a ratio warning, a 40-row table, a medical form, an invoice, and a parent message without becoming generic or childish?

The final comparison now includes `docs/redesign/finalist-identity-worlds.md`,
which tests both finalists through the same approved logo seed, care meanings,
application jobs, and decision criteria before production selection.

## Research Operating System

### Lane 1: Audience and category meaning

Study the manager's emotional and operational tensions, direct-competitor codes, and the language used for trust, care, control, and evidence.

**Outputs:** tension map, category-code audit, anti-code list, positioning options, proof points.

**Gate:** every direction must solve the same audience tension through a different credible belief.

### Lane 2: Distinctive identity assets

Study how excellent brands build recognition from a small set of governed assets rather than an unlimited moodboard: wordmark, symbol, type behavior, color hierarchy, shape language, imagery, voice, and sound/motion.

**Outputs:** asset inventory, identity construction rules, scale tests, one-color tests, forbidden uses.

**Gate:** each direction needs at least two ownable assets beyond a palette.

### Lane 3: Typography and color

Test display and product faces separately. Evaluate numerals, tables, small labels, long copy, multilingual fallback, licensing, loading, and 200% text. Separate brand, interaction, semantic, categorical, and data color roles.

**Outputs:** type specimens, role matrix, palette proportions, contrast results, color-collision register.

**Gate:** color never carries meaning alone, and the product remains legible when brand color is removed.

### Lane 4: Product and flow behavior

Use Mobbin and live products to study hierarchy, progressive disclosure, return paths, status feedback, customization, empty/error/offline states, and cross-device continuity. Extract behavior, never surface styling alone.

**Outputs:** flow evidence sheets, borrow/adapt/reject notes, component implications.

**Gate:** a direction cannot score well because its marketing frame is beautiful while its forms and records are ordinary.

### Lane 5: Motion and interaction character

Define what moves, why it moves, how it settles, interruption behavior, keyboard/focus continuity, and reduced-motion equivalents. Motion must communicate continuity, response, state, or meaningful completion.

**Outputs:** motion principles, timing and spring families, signature transition, reduced-motion map.

**Gate:** no looping decoration, counter roulette, ambient drift, or celebration in high-risk work.

### Lane 6: Stress and selection

Render identical nursery content in every system. Test calm, forecast, critical, unknown, completion, dense records, long copy, mobile, tablet, keyboard, contrast, forced colors, RTL, and 200% text.

**Outputs:** controlled brand boards, product specimens, browser evidence, scorecard, explicit tradeoffs.

**Gate:** no production tokens or page migration before the owner selects one direction or a precisely defined hybrid.

## Source Stack

### Process and system construction

- [Design Council service-design methods](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/fileadmin/uploads/dc/Documents/DesignCouncil_Design%20methods%20for%20developing%20services.pdf): diverge and converge around evidence rather than committing to the first attractive solution.
- [Notion on building a design system](https://www.notion.com/blog/how-to-create-a-design-system): visual audit, design language, reusable library, accessibility, and living documentation.
- [Headspace design-system case study](https://www.notion.com/customers/headspace): keep mission, principles, standards, and iteration in one living source of truth.
- [GOV.UK Design System styles](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/): consistent type, spacing, color roles, and service patterns for high-trust work.

### Identity and brand world

- [Duolingo identity color](https://design.duolingo.com/identity/color): one core brand color, governed secondary colors, and neutral utility hierarchy.
- [Duolingo typography](https://design.duolingo.com/identity/typography): distinct display and body roles with explicit size and combination rules.
- [Duolingo shape language](https://design.duolingo.com/illustration/shape-language): recognition through a constrained construction grammar, rhythm, and simplicity.
- [Duolingo imagery](https://design.duolingo.com/identity/imagery): illustration and photography receive different jobs inside one identity.

### Product craft and accessibility

- [Apple design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles): start from human purpose and make the few important things excellent.
- [Apple color guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color): use color consistently for brand, continuity, status, and feedback.
- [Apple typography guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography): legibility and hierarchy precede stylistic display.
- [Apple motion guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion): motion communicates status, feedback, instruction, and continuity.
- [Apple accessibility guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility): support larger text, contrast, comfortable targets, and reduced motion.
- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) and [Use of Color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color): color-independent meaning, contrast, focus, and adaptable interaction.

### Current product-flow evidence

- [Duolingo progress screen](https://mobbin.com/screens/821e9808-bc92-4c14-8003-d32191db87e5): branded progress and one explicit next action.
- [Headspace daily sequence](https://mobbin.com/screens/ec5baa02-aace-497d-ac04-bfa0557e1bdd): white canvas, emotionally specific media, and a guided sequence.
- [Notion project workspace](https://mobbin.com/screens/944bb388-3263-4025-b75b-46e77a564985): content-first hierarchy, contextual controls, and restrained chrome.
- [Revolut account overview](https://mobbin.com/screens/44da0e87-2f76-4f85-ab9d-bfca747da29a): broad capability compressed into a clear primary object and contextual actions.

## Evidence Rules

- A screenshot is evidence of composition, not proof of usability, motion, accessibility, or performance.
- A competitor feature is evidence of category expectation, not permission to copy its visual language.
- A brand reference must be annotated with the asset or behavior worth learning from.
- Every recommendation names the tradeoff and kill criterion.
- No chart appears without a real decision, source, timeframe, and truthful scale.
- No direction receives a higher score until it has been tested against identical content and states.

## Phase Sequence

1. **Reset:** preserve product strategy, reject the old recommendation, and freeze production visuals.
2. **Diverge:** define six strategic and visual systems.
3. **Brand proof:** render identity, type, color, voice, imagery, and motion signatures.
4. **Product proof:** apply each system to the same small set of high-value components and states.
5. **Stress:** run accessibility, density, localization, and cross-device checks.
6. **Select:** choose one system, reject all others, or define a narrow hybrid with named source rules.
7. **Codify:** write the final brand constitution, tokens, component APIs, imagery rules, and motion grammar.
8. **Pilot:** redesign Today plus one dense record and one high-risk workflow before app-wide rollout.

The completed weighted evaluation and research shortlist live in
`docs/redesign/brand-direction-evaluation.md` and
`/design-lab/brand-directions/evaluation`. The scorecard ranks potential; it
does not select production tokens.

The completed identical two-finalist proof lives in
`docs/redesign/finalist-proof-round.md` and
`/design-lab/brand-directions/finalists`. It tests operations, a high-trust
record and form, and a parent-safe projection without changing source content or
behavior. Kinetic Kindness is the research lead; Living Record remains the
evidence-led alternative; neither is locked for production.

Pre-lock font, script, delivery, 200% specimen, mixed-direction, and semantic
color due diligence lives in
`docs/redesign/finalist-type-color-readiness.md` and the readiness appendix on
the finalist route. The current Latin families are OFL and production-feasible,
but neither finalist has a selected Arabic family.

| Criterion | Weight | Reject below |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Strategic distinctiveness | 20% | 3.5 / 5 |
| Immediate Kiddz recognition | 15% | 3.5 / 5 |
| Manager trust and clarity | 20% | 4 / 5 |
| Emotional warmth without infantilization | 10% | 3.5 / 5 |
| Dense-product survival | 15% | 4 / 5 |
| Motion and interaction coherence | 5% | 3.5 / 5 |
| Accessibility and localization | 10% | 4 / 5 |
| Cross-device continuity | 5% | 4 / 5 |

The score supports judgment; it does not replace it. No recommendation is active until the controlled prototypes exist and the product owner chooses.

# Kiddz Online Creative Territory Briefs

**Status:** Build-ready briefs v1
**Last updated:** 2026-07-10
**Shared strategy:** Care, visibly handled.
**Territories:** Daylight, Signal, Carebook

## Purpose

These briefs define three genuinely different creative systems for the same product strategy. Each territory will be built with the same realistic nursery content and functional anatomy so the comparison is about design judgment, not different data or different features.

None of the territories is selected. Production UI remains untouched until the territories are browser-tested, scored, compared, and reviewed.

## Shared Product Scenario

Each territory must render the same synthetic manager state at 09:18 on a live Tuesday:

- Branch: Riverside Nursery.
- Four rooms: Nest, Meadow, Orchard, and Studio.
- 47 children expected; 41 present; 3 absent; 2 late; 1 unknown.
- 12 staff expected; 11 present; one absence affecting Meadow.
- Nest is safe.
- Meadow is safe now but forecast below ratio at 12:30 when a qualified practitioner starts break.
- Orchard is waiting on one child attendance state.
- Studio is safe.
- One submitted accident requires manager review and parent acknowledgment.
- Four daily care reports remain incomplete.
- One family payment of EUR 240 is recorded but unallocated.

No chart or trend may appear unless this scenario contains the required source data. The territory Today view should use state, room comparison, timeline, counts, and work items before analytical visualization.

## Shared Screen Set

Every territory prototype includes:

1. Manager Today at 1440 x 900.
2. Room state expansion or inspect panel.
3. Owned work queue with critical, forecast, required, and informational states.
4. Children or attendance table with realistic density.
5. Daily-care or incident form with draft and validation behavior.
6. High-risk review or confirmation.
7. Significant low-risk completion state.
8. Tablet room view at 1024 x 768.
9. Mobile companion crop at 390 x 844.
10. Reduced-motion representation.

## Shared Non-Negotiables

- Approximately 90% white or near-white operational canvas.
- Desktop-first hierarchy, intentionally recomposed for tablet and mobile.
- One readable operational source of truth.
- Color has a documented state, action, category, or brand job.
- Warm charcoal anchors the system.
- Important state is understandable without color.
- No fake or unnecessary charts.
- No random decorative shapes, orbs, blobs, gradients, or colored left borders.
- No equal rainbow card grid.
- No generic `View More`, `Done`, or `Action needed` labels.
- No illustration in active safety, medical, financial, permission, or destructive state.
- Motion explains continuity or completion and always settles.
- Tables, forms, and evidence views receive the same craft as Today.
- Existing legacy/native/parity behavior remains a future implementation constraint.

## Territory 1: Daylight

### Strategic interpretation

**Care becomes easier to act on when the day feels open, warm, and visible.**

Daylight is the most expressive and emotionally immediate territory. It translates the approved logo palette and Headspace-like emotional clarity into a disciplined adult product. The operational canvas is bright and quiet; important moments receive confident fields of color, warm illustration, and satisfying settle motion.

### Emotional goal

The manager should feel welcomed by an optimistic product that immediately gets serious about the day. It should feel energetic without feeling busy, friendly without feeling childish, and colorful without becoming a rainbow dashboard.

### Primary references

- Headspace: color and illustration attached to a specific emotional moment.
- Duolingo: governed brand family and recognizable motion, without gamification.
- Apple: consequence-matched feedback and tactile settle.
- Klarna: bold brand fields separated from transactional utility.
- Approved Kiddz Online logo intro: warm canvas, high-chroma constellation, physical return to order.

### Palette hypothesis

| Role | Candidate | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operational canvas | `#FFFCF7` | Most page background |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` | Tools, forms, tables, menus |
| Warm ink | `#2F2A25` | Primary text and icon anchor |
| Secondary ink | `#716A63` | Supporting text, never critical state |
| Digital action blue | `#2E6BFF` | Primary safe action and selection |
| Kiddz orange | `#F47A32` | Brand ownership and selected warm moments |
| Joy yellow | `#F4C33B` | Brand highlight, not warning by default |
| Human pink | `#E96B9A` | Brand/guidance, not error |
| Fresh teal | `#2FB7AD` | Brand/guidance, not automatic success |
| Growth green | `#4FA554` | Brand/guidance; semantic success token remains separate |
| Imagination purple | `#9164CB` | Brand/guidance and selected categories |

Semantic danger, warning, success, info, unknown, and historical tokens are derived and tested separately. The brand six do not each receive a dashboard card.

### Typography

- Wordmark: approved Fredoka `Kiddz` + Inter `Online`.
- Product: Inter across navigation, data, forms, and records.
- Display moments: Fredoka at restrained weights for major positive state, onboarding, and low-risk guidance.
- Numbers: Inter tabular figures with compact numeric hierarchy.
- High-risk state: Inter only; no friendly display face.

### Geometry

- Operational cards: 8px radius maximum.
- Controls: 6-8px radius; pills only for tags, status, or compact segmented modes.
- Brand/guidance fields may use larger organic corner relationships when they are not tool containers.
- A full color field may own a significant positive or forecast state; it cannot be added to fill empty space.
- Shadow is reserved for sheets, drag, focus, and lifted inspect panels.

### Illustration

Simple, bold, flat illustrations built from a controlled family of soft forms and warm ink expressions. They appear in onboarding, empty guidance, parent education, training, and meaningful low-risk completion.

Today contains no decorative character. A small brand mark may appear in a verified all-clear state only if it does not compete with room state or source freshness.

### Motion signature

**Bright settle:** A changed object receives a brief 1-2% scale/position response, color resolves, and surrounding geometry settles without bounce loops.

- Press: immediate 80-120ms response.
- Panel continuity: restrained spring around 260-340ms.
- Significant completion: source state morph plus one brief brand-color pulse.
- Brand milestone: anticipation and soft bounce can be richer.
- Reduced motion: cross-fade and immediate state replacement.

### Today composition

- Near-white page with one concise readiness statement: `Safe now. Two things need handling before lunch.`
- Readiness may use a wide warm color field only because it is the primary state object, not a hero card.
- Room comparison remains structured and white, with small state markers and one larger color moment on the forecast-risk row.
- Work queue uses white rows; priority changes icon, label, timing, and selective background tint.
- Secondary completion and finance state remain below live room state.

### Table treatment

- White bounded table with warm ink, stable identity, restrained separators, and strong selected row.
- Saved-view and filter chips use color only when the chip explains an active state.
- Avatars or room tokens can use brand colors for recognition, never severity.
- Bulk mode changes the toolbar and selection field rather than adding more icons to every row.

### Form treatment

- White form on warm canvas with compact sections and occasional colored guidance field.
- Factual fields start empty.
- Conditional evidence expands in place.
- Draft state appears in a stable action region.
- Validation uses direct semantic language and no character illustration.

### High-risk state

Warmth retreats. A full-width, pale semantic field names the affected room/child, current consequence, source freshness, and action. Direct Inter typography and standard icons lead. Brand orange, pink, yellow, teal, green, and purple are absent unless one is also the documented semantic token.

### Completion state

For a low-risk meaningful completion, the source row resolves and a compact color field can briefly say `Opening check confirmed at 09:21`. It offers `Review evidence` and the exact correction action. No confetti.

### Cross-device character

- Tablet uses larger white room rows and the same concentrated color moments.
- Mobile first viewport shows current room state and one primary action; brand color appears in the action and meaningful state, not in five navigation tabs.
- Parent surfaces may use more illustration and color than manager evidence surfaces.

### Strengths

- Closest to the approved logo and the user's Headspace-oriented references.
- Most immediately ownable as a childcare brand.
- Strong emotional range for parent, onboarding, and guidance experiences.
- Gives the product room for award-level motion and illustration.

### Risks

- Color can become juvenile or semantically confused.
- Fredoka can spread too far into tools.
- Expressive fields can become marketing cards.
- Warm canvas and shadows can reduce long-session precision if not restrained.

### Kill criteria

Reject Daylight if the dense table, incident form, or ratio-risk state feels less authoritative than the Signal equivalent, or if users describe it as childish, wellness-like, or visually busy.

## Territory 2: Signal

### Strategic interpretation

**Care becomes trustworthy when every change has a clear signal, source, and next transition.**

Signal is the most operational and system-driven territory. It draws from Flighty's live-state clarity, Stripe's durable transaction objects, Apple's feedback hierarchy, and Vercel's geometry. Kiddz personality comes through concentrated color, direct language, and kinetic continuity rather than illustration inside the manager workspace.

### Emotional goal

The manager should feel they are using a beautifully tuned live instrument: fast, exact, readable, and quietly alive. It must remain human through warmth of language and color, not become a developer console.

### Primary references

- Flighty: rich live truth, changed values, timing, confidence, and source reporting.
- Stripe: one object, timeline, consequence, and reversible states.
- Apple: integrated feedback, focus, and severity-matched interruption.
- Vercel/Geist: token discipline, grid, and state completeness.
- Things: progressive detail and source continuity.

### Palette hypothesis

| Role | Candidate | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canvas | `#FBFBFA` | Operational field |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` | Tools and raised context |
| Core ink | `#1F2022` | Primary text and icons |
| Secondary ink | `#676A70` | Metadata and annotation |
| Structural line | `#E5E6E3` | Dividers and bounded workspaces |
| Signal blue | `#185CFF` | Selection, primary safe action, active data |
| Signal mint | `#0B9B72` | Confirmed safe/success when semantic rules allow |
| Signal amber | `#D98700` | Forecast and preventive attention |
| Signal red | `#D43D49` | Current critical consequence |
| Kiddz orange | `#F47A32` | Brand signature outside semantic collision |
| Signal violet | `#7456C9` | Review/pending or specialist category after testing |

The system uses fewer simultaneous hues than Daylight. The approved multi-color identity remains in brand assets and parent/guidance moments.

### Typography

- Wordmark: approved Fredoka + Inter.
- Product: Inter, with high attention to optical size, tabular figures, time, currency, and status labels.
- Fredoka is limited to wordmark and rare brand milestones.
- Numeric state can be prominent but is always paired with a plain-language result.
- Monospace is reserved for technical identifiers in audit/admin views, never normal operational copy.

### Geometry

- 4-6px operational radii; 8px maximum for cards and panels.
- Crisp bounded data workspaces and aligned baselines.
- Status pills are compact and sparse.
- Timelines, connected changes, and comparison tracks are functional geometry, not decorative diagrams.
- Elevation is minimal; panel layering relies on border, focus, and small shadow.

### Illustration

No illustration in the primary manager workspace. Brand and parent experiences can inherit the approved illustration language, but Signal itself uses icon, typography, timing, and data geometry.

Empty states use concise language and a useful action. A small custom object icon is acceptable when it identifies a domain, not when it decorates space.

### Motion signature

**Signal handoff:** The affected object carries its identity through list, panel, action, and return. Movement is direct, low-bounce, and source-oriented.

- Press/focus: 70-100ms.
- Inspect panel: 180-240ms with clear source origin.
- Row reorder/assignment: 220-300ms spring with destination preview.
- Live update: value change and old/new relationship, no counter roulette.
- Reduced motion: immediate panel/state swap with persistent focus.

### Today composition

- Readiness appears as a direct statement beside freshness and scope, not inside a colorful hero.
- The room operating plane is the visual center: rows align current children, staff, ratio, next change, and owner.
- A thin time axis or forecast lane is allowed because it represents actual upcoming breaks and arrivals.
- Work queue sits beside or beneath the room plane and uses priority, due time, and lifecycle state.
- Brand color is concentrated in selected state and action; semantic colors dominate only when meaning requires them.

### Table treatment

- Best-in-class dense workspace with frozen identity, column presets, in-table overflow, and inspect panel.
- Row height modes support office density and touch review.
- Status, time, and source align for scan speed.
- The selected row receives a stable neutral/blue field, not a shadow card.
- Event history can open as a connected timeline with changed values.

### Form treatment

- Form fields align to a strict grid with readable section hierarchy.
- Summary and consequence remain visible in a side panel on desktop when useful.
- Conditional fields open adjacent to the trigger.
- Server validation and revision conflict return to the exact field/object.
- Primary action names the transition and amount/count.

### High-risk state

Signal is strongest here. Current critical state uses a compact semantic field, explicit `Now` label, source timestamp, rule/cause, owner, and one resolution action. Forecast amber never looks identical to current red. The state remains in the room plane until verified.

### Completion state

Completion changes the row, timeline, and queue together. A stable feedback region names the server-confirmed result and correction path. Motion is shorter than Daylight and contains no illustration or celebratory color expansion.

### Cross-device character

- Tablet preserves the room plane as a compact roster/time view.
- Mobile becomes an exact live card/row for current room or assigned work, inspired by Live Activities but not copied from iOS chrome.
- Widgets or notifications are plausible future projections because the state model is concise and explainable.

### Strengths

- Strongest operational confidence and dense desktop quality.
- Best fit for live ratios, staffing, finance, medical, and audit workflows.
- Lowest risk of decorative drift.
- Motion can feel sophisticated through continuity rather than spectacle.

### Risks

- Can become cold, technical, or indistinguishable from developer tools.
- Limited illustration may underuse approved Kiddz brand equity.
- Dense geometry may intimidate nontechnical managers.
- Blue-led interaction can feel generic if orange and wordmark behavior do not create enough ownership.

### Kill criteria

Reject Signal if users cannot feel the childcare context without reading the logo, if the manager workspace resembles a fintech/DevOps dashboard, or if practitioners find the room surface too clinical.

## Territory 3: Carebook

### Strategic interpretation

**Care becomes meaningful when the day reads as a shared record, not a database.**

Carebook is the most editorial and tactile territory. It treats the nursery day as a living record made by people: observations, handovers, decisions, acknowledgments, and evidence. The product uses paper-like hierarchy, expressive type, annotated state, and layered continuity without imitating a notebook or adding craft decoration to every surface.

### Emotional goal

The manager should feel that complex records are humane, readable, and connected to a story of care. It should feel crafted and memorable while staying adult, contemporary, and fast.

### Primary references

- Notion: content-first hierarchy and in-context depth.
- Airbnb: durable object detail and human context around commitments.
- Headspace: approachable emotional communication.
- Cosmos: provenance, curation, and return to source.
- Editorial identity references in the Pinterest board: bold type and controlled color on warm space.

### Palette hypothesis

| Role | Candidate | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canvas | `#FCF8F1` | Warm editorial field |
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Records, forms, tables, sheets |
| Ink | `#302B27` | Primary text and line work |
| Pencil | `#746D65` | Annotation and metadata |
| Vermilion | `#F05F36` | Core brand/action candidate |
| Raspberry | `#D9577C` | Human/communication brand role |
| Mustard | `#DFA71B` | Brand emphasis, separate warning token |
| Ocean | `#287D88` | Digital/communication accent |
| Moss | `#5D864A` | Care/growth category |
| Lilac | `#8669B2` | Review/specialist category after testing |

Carebook uses muted-but-confident color more often as labels, headings, seals, or focused fields than as large dashboard backgrounds. Semantic colors remain distinct and higher-clarity.

### Typography

- Wordmark: approved Fredoka + Inter.
- Product controls/data: Inter.
- Editorial layer candidate: a carefully tested humanist serif such as Newsreader for narrative summaries, parent letters, and selected insight headings only.
- Fredoka remains identity/positive display, not the main editorial face.
- Dense tools remain Inter to protect scan speed.

The extra editorial face is a territory hypothesis, not an approved dependency. If it weakens localization, accessibility, or product coherence, it is removed.

### Geometry

- Paper sections are unframed or bounded by quiet rules before becoming cards.
- Cards use 6-8px radius and only when an object needs a contained preview.
- Sheets can layer with a subtle offset/shadow when they represent a temporary detail or handoff.
- Labels and tabs may use color-filled compact shapes, but no colored left border convention.
- Organic geometry is reserved for brand/illustration, not form controls.

### Illustration

Editorial spot illustration can accompany guidance, parent communication, empty records, training, and low-risk milestones. It uses a limited cut-paper/ink language with consistent shapes and no stock scenes.

Operational Today uses no ambient illustration. The story comes from real names, times, room changes, notes, and source history.

### Motion signature

**Record to record:** Lists, details, and history behave like connected layers. A selected item lifts into focus, edits settle back into the record, and handoff moves the object to its next owner.

- Focus/press: 90-120ms.
- Sheet lift/return: 240-320ms with subtle scale and vertical continuity.
- Timeline insertion: 180-240ms reveal at the actual chronological position.
- Completion: annotation resolves into a dated record/seal, then returns to the source.
- Reduced motion: opacity and immediate focus change.

No fake page curl, handwriting animation, paper texture movement, or scrapbook effects.

### Today composition

- Opens with an editorial morning brief: `Riverside is safe now. Meadow needs cover before 12:30.`
- The brief is text-led and linked to its source facts.
- Room state appears as a structured running list with time, people, and one next change.
- Work queue feels like assigned notes in the day record, with owner, due time, and lifecycle state.
- Recent resolved work can appear as a compact chronological strip, making progress visible without celebration.

### Table treatment

- Dense data remains in a bounded Inter table.
- Record preview uses editorial hierarchy: identity, current state, recent change, source, and history.
- Column controls are compact and progressive.
- Notes and human communication are more readable than in Signal, without increasing every row height.

### Form treatment

- Form reads as a structured record with a clear opening summary and section rhythm.
- Narrative fields receive appropriate space and typographic care.
- Evidence, attachments, and history feel connected to the record rather than separate utility widgets.
- Draft and author context remain visible.
- Review step creates a readable summary of what will enter the permanent record.

### High-risk state

Editorial character becomes precise. A high-risk review resembles a formal but humane notice: exact title, affected person/room, facts, missing evidence, next transition, and durable history. Serif display, illustration, seals, and decorative labels are excluded.

### Completion state

A low-risk completion inserts a dated, attributed entry into the day record and briefly emphasizes it. The feedback language focuses on what is now documented and who can see it.

### Cross-device character

- Tablet is especially strong for care and handover records: one focused sheet over a clear roster.
- Mobile uses readable summaries and capture sheets, preserving the source record and author.
- Parent experience can use more editorial warmth and illustration while sharing type, ink, and color ownership.

### Strengths

- Most distinctive alternative to generic SaaS while keeping a near-white product.
- Strong fit for child profiles, care reports, handovers, communication, evidence, and parent trust.
- Makes provenance and history emotionally legible.
- Offers rich editorial brand expression without requiring colored dashboard cards.

### Risks

- Can become slow, precious, or document-heavy.
- Extra serif face can fragment the system and complicate localization.
- Paper metaphors can become decorative skeuomorphism.
- Live ratio and staffing comparison may be less immediate than Signal.

### Kill criteria

Reject Carebook if room comparison, high-volume attendance, finance, or manager triage becomes slower; if the interface resembles a journal app; or if editorial styling overtakes operational state.

## Comparative Hypothesis

| Dimension | Daylight | Signal | Carebook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emotional warmth | Highest | Moderate | High |
| Live operational precision | High if disciplined | Highest | Moderate/high |

## Build Order

1. Create a shared synthetic scenario and content model.
2. Build one neutral functional territory shell to prove identical information anatomy.
3. Apply Daylight tokens, typography, geometry, illustration placeholder rules, and motion.
4. Apply Signal independently from the neutral shell.
5. Apply Carebook independently from the neutral shell.
6. Verify each at 1440 x 900, 1280 x 800, 1024 x 768, 768 x 1024, and 390 x 844.
7. Capture Today, table, form, high-risk, completion, tablet, and mobile evidence.
8. Test keyboard, zoom, reduced motion, color contrast, and source-state continuity.
9. Score without normalizing away genuine differences.
10. Recommend one direction, identify any justified hybrid elements, and wait for selection before production rollout.

## Scoring Method

Use the territory scorecard from `brand-expression-synthesis.md`:

- Operational clarity: 25%.
- Distinctive brand ownership: 20%.
- Emotional fit: 15%.
- Dense-surface survival: 15%.
- Motion coherence: 10%.
- Accessibility: 10%.
- Cross-device continuity: 5%.

Operational clarity or accessibility below 4/5 rejects a territory regardless of weighted total.

## Review Questions

1. Which territory makes the manager understand the branch fastest?
2. Which feels most unmistakably Kiddz rather than a reference product?
3. Which uses the approved logo palette without rainbow equality?
4. Which makes a table and long form feel crafted rather than themed?
5. Which high-risk state is clearest and most trustworthy?
6. Which completion feels satisfying without slowing repeat work?
7. Which tablet room view remains usable under interruption?
8. Which mobile crop removes the right decisions?
9. Which system can scale through all 1,713 parity obligations?
10. Which risks can be corrected without destroying the territory's central idea?

The territory recommendation must answer these questions with browser evidence, not taste adjectives alone.
